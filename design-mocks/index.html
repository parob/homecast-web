Section 1

Toasts

Two toast systems are mounted at once today — Sonner (top-centre pill) and the shadcn/Radix Toaster (top on mobile, bottom-right on desktop). Same app, two shapes, two positions. 355 call sites, 223 of them toast.error. Fire each one and compare.

Current — Sonner pill

Live

Top-centre, rounded-full, bg-background/80, transparent border. Fine for one short word. The moment a title, an icon and a description share it, the 9999px radius turns it into a lozenge and the text sits in the pinched part.

Breaks on: descriptions, long names, stacking, and any wallpaper light enough to swallow an 80%-opaque white pill with no border.

Current — Radix Toaster

Also live

The second system, still wired in App.tsx:195. Stock shadcn: p-6, 420px, hard border, bottom-right on desktop. Used by Account and History settings only.

Recommendation: delete it. Port the ~8 call sites to Sonner and drop @radix-ui/react-toast.

A · Bottom-right card

Sonner default

What Vercel, Linear, GitHub and Stripe do. Sonner's own default, essentially unstyled. Stacks and collapses, out of the way of the header, never over the content you just acted on.

Cost: delete the classNames override. On phone it moves to bottom-centre above the tab bar.

B · Capsule HUD

Apple

Keeps the pill you already have, but enforces what a pill is for: one short line, no description, ever. This is the iOS volume/AirPods HUD. Glass material, so it matches the tab bar and widgets rather than shadcn.

Catch: needs a lint rule or a wrapper — a description passed to a pill has nowhere to go.

C · Glass card, bottom-centre

Recommended

The app's own material-thick and rounded-2xl — the same surface as the tab bar, the header bubble and every widget. Holds a description without deforming, and reads as Homecast rather than as a component library.

Sits above the tab bar, so it never covers the thing you just tapped, and never fights the header.

D · Accent rail

Slack / Notion

Solid popover surface with a 3px status rail. Severity is carried by a stripe rather than by flooding the toast with red — 223 error toasts in a saturated red is a lot of alarm for things like "View-only access".

Best if you want toasts legible over any wallpaper without relying on backdrop blur.

E · Collapsing island

Dynamic Island

One line collapsed; tap to expand the detail. Repeats of the same message coalesce into a count instead of stacking. Directly targets the flapping-relay case toast-bus.ts already debounces by hand.

Most work of the five, and only pays off because so many toasts here are repeats.

F · Don't toast at all

Apply everywhere

The real fix for a lot of the 223. When a write fails, the widget that failed says so — a red ring and a line of text on the tile. Apple's rule: feedback belongs where the action happened. A toast is for things with no on-screen anchor.

Not a replacement — a companion. Keep toasts for scene runs, deletes, saves and connection state.
The convention, briefly.
  • Material 3 puts snackbars bottom-centre on mobile, bottom-left on desktop; one at a time; one action; never for errors that need a decision.
  • Apple has no toast primitive at all. Confirmation is the control changing state; transient banners are reserved for things arriving from outside.
  • Web apps (Vercel, Linear, GitHub, Stripe, Notion, Figma) are near-unanimous on a bottom corner, small card, 3–5 max, hover to expand.
  • Top-centre is for things happening to you — a dropped connection, an incoming call. It's the wrong shelf for "Group renamed".
Section 2

Menu morphed into its button

Today the menu is a detached popover 4px below a ghost button, zooming in from 95%. Four ways to weld the two into one surface, with the trigger going white while open. Click the ⋮ on each card.

Current

Live

Detached. sideOffset=4, bordered popover, zoom-95. The button stays ghost-transparent, so nothing on screen says which button opened this.

The 4px gap and the border are what make it read as two objects.

M1 · Welded corner

Closest to the ask

Button goes white and square-bottomed; the menu's top-right corner squares off to meet it. One continuous white surface with a notch where the button sits. Wipes open from the button's edge.

Radix: sideOffset={0}, align="end", plus a data-[state=open] variant on the trigger.

M2 · Grows out of the button

Most literal morph

The menu is the button, unfolding. It starts at exactly 30×30 in the button's own box and expands down-left; the dots stay drawn in the corner throughout.

Note: animating width/height can't use a Radix content animation — needs the menu positioned over the trigger.

M3 · Dots rotate, panel unrolls

Cheapest

Same weld, but the trigger's glyph rotates 90° to a row of dots (or an ✕) so the button reads as a toggle. Menu unrolls on the Y axis rather than zooming.

~15 lines of CSS on the existing components. No layout change.

M4 · iOS context menu

Heaviest

The rest of the screen dims and blurs, the card lifts, the menu scales up from the button. What long-press does on iOS. Strong, and right for destructive menus — but a lot of ceremony for "Rename".

Worth it for the accessory long-press menu; too much for every card.
One caution on the white button. Over a light wallpaper a white trigger on a white glass card has almost no edge — it reads as a hole. All four variants above rely on the menu's shadow to carry the shape. Worth checking M1 against the Warm wallpaper before committing.
Section 3

Tab bar

Fixed 64px slots, a 20px icon over a 10px label wrapped to two lines, and an active state that is a flat translucent block. The two-line labels are what make it ragged — five pins of different name lengths give five different label heights inside one pill.

Current

Live

w-16 · text-[10px] · line-clamp-2 · active = bg-black/10. Note how "Living Room" and "Everything Off" wrap while "Study" doesn't — the icon row stays level but the pill grows to the tallest label.

Measured 352×69. On a 375px phone that is edge to edge, and the 69px height is set by the one label that wrapped.

T1 · Expanding active tab

Recommended

Inactive tabs are icon-only; the active one grows sideways to show its label in a filled capsule. The bar shrinks from 352×69 to 281×50, labels never wrap, and the active tab is unmistakable. Click through them.

Trade: you lose the labels on inactive tabs. Fine for 4–5 pins the user chose; poor for 5 similar rooms.

T2 · Material 3 indicator

Android-native

Keeps every label, but the active pill sits behind the icon only and the label goes semibold below it. Labels forced to one line with an ellipsis. This is the Android system nav bar, so it will feel native in the Tauri build.

Keeps all five labels visible. Same 352px width, but 65px tall instead of 69px because nothing wraps.

T3 · Tighter, tinted

Smallest change

The current bar with three edits: one-line labels with ellipsis, 56px slots, and an active state tinted primary instead of grey. Same structure, same code path, most of the raggedness gone. Measures 312×58.

~6 lines in MobileTabBar.tsx. Ships today.

T4 · Icons only

Most minimal

No labels at all; a primary underline marks the active tab. Bar drops to 246×48 — the smallest of the five. Leans entirely on icon recognition — viable because the user pinned these themselves.

Risk: two rooms with the same derived icon become indistinguishable. Needs a long-press tooltip.
Whichever you pick, two things are worth fixing in all of them: the label and icon are both muted-foreground on an inactive tab, which over a light wallpaper is grey-on-grey; and the active background is a neutral wash carrying none of the app's blue. T1/T2/T3 all address both.
Section 4

Other things worth a pass

Found while reading the surrounding code. Ordered by payoff for effort — say which you want mocked and I'll add them here.

Delete the second toast system Small

App.tsx mounts both <Toaster/> and <Sonner/>. Eight call sites use the Radix one; everything else uses Sonner.

Removes a whole component family, a dependency, and the possibility of two visually different toasts on screen at once.

Error toasts don't say what failed Small

"Failed to save", "Failed to delete", "Failed to reorder groups" — 200+ of these carry no object and no cause.

"Couldn't rename Kitchen — relay offline" is the same amount of code and actually tells the user whether to retry.

Destructive confirmations are text-only Small

Delete flows go through a plain AlertDialog. No icon, no name of the thing, no count of what goes with it.

"Delete Kitchen?" with the 6 accessories it contains listed underneath prevents the mistake rather than reporting it.

Header bubble vs. buttons over dark wallpaper Medium

AppHeader already toggles the bubble off on dark backgrounds, with a comment about a 56px slab beside two 40px circles. The seam is still visible mid-tone.

One consistent control height and a single glass surface would remove the conditional entirely.

Empty states Medium

"Empty collection" is a caption. Rooms, scenes and automations with nothing in them mostly render as blank space.

An empty grid is the first thing a new user sees. It's the cheapest place to put the primary action.

Focus rings are the same blue as selection Small

--ring and --primary are both 217 91% 60%, so a keyboard focus ring and a selected ring are indistinguishable.

Already bitten once per the project notes. Shifting --ring a few degrees separates them permanently.
