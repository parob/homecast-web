The status bubble

One dot in the header opens a w-[280px] p-3 popover — src/components/layout/StatusBadge.tsx. Its body is up to three stacked sections: ConnectionSection, LocalModeSection, RelaySection.

Four things are wrong with it, none of them a fault. It is an opaque card on a glass app — the one white rectangle on the screen. Its headline repeats the dot: a green dot followed by "Connection is good" says the same thing twice, and the only new information, 32ms, is demoted to the smaller grey line. It is a 280px card carrying one line in the healthy state, where there is no Reconnect button, no relay section and no Local Mode section — so it is a large empty box that covers the Scenes and Automations pills. And nothing ties it to what you tapped: no tail, align="end" against a mid-header trigger, two thirds of the width on a 440px phone.

Every option now carries the chain. The first pass had it only in C, which made the choice "diagnosis or sleekness". It is not: device → cloud → relay → home is four pips and three lines, and it fits in a header pill. So it is in all five, drawn in whichever style the Nodes / Bar / Dots / Rail switch is set to — that switch drives every option at once, the same way the state picker does. C is no longer "the one with the chain"; it is the one built around it, where the chain is the content rather than a band above it.

The last four states exist to answer when the chain is worth it. In each, today's dot and sentence are not just terse, they point somewhere wrong: Relay unreachable says "your home is not responding" when the phone and internet are fine and the Mac is the problem; Mac answering slowly says "your connection is slow" about a 28ms connection; Community · same Wi-Fi has no cloud in the path at all; Community · over a tunnel is slow at the tunnel and 8ms at the Mac behind it. One dot cannot separate any of those pairs.
Two decisions these options must not unpick. This one bubble is a deliberate merge of three pills that contradicted each other — a red "Offline" beside a green "Local Mode" while the home worked fine through the second. And the indicator is present at every state, including good, because one that appears only when something is wrong cannot be told apart from one that is broken or one that was never measuring. Rationale at StatusBadge.tsx:1–30 and lib/status-badge.ts:1–22. Every option below keeps the dot always visible; what varies is the box.

Now that the chain is in all five, the choice is no longer "diagnosis or sleekness" — it is where the chain sits in the hierarchy. C puts it first and the reading second. A and E put the reading first and the chain second. B puts a four-pip version in the header and everything else behind a tap. D puts it above history. On the evidence of the last four states, the hop sentence is more often the useful half than the number is — "Homecast can't reach your Mac" beats "9s" — which argues for C's ordering, in A's material, at B's resting size. That hybrid is buildable: A's card, C's hierarchy, and B's header pips for the healthy state.
