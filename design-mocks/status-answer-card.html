Homecast · the status popover

One question, one answer

Tap the dot and you are asking does my home work? The popover answers with four reports, one per component. This is the case for replacing them with one card that answers the question — and shows the chain only when something in it is broken.

What a person is asking

  1. Does County Hall work right now? Yes, yes-but, or no.
  2. If not — why, and is it me? One sentence. Whether there is anything to do.
  3. Has it been like this? A number, and a way to the full story.

What the popover shows today

  • Connection — the chain, a sentence, the round trip quality + serving → buildChain()
  • Reliability — live status, two percentages, a strip, the last outage GET_HOME_UPTIME (the server's probe)
  • Local Mode — a reason, an identity caveat, a link useLocalMode()
  • Relay Status — a pill, a paragraph, six stats, a sparkline, three counts relaySectionState() + serverConnection + HomeKit.getStats()

Each section is an honest rendering of its own source. That is exactly the trouble: the sources agree with each other now (HomeServing saw to that), but the popover still lets each one speak. Your second screenshot — cloud relay down, this Mac standing in — is the whole argument in one frame:

  • Three tellings of who is serving County Hall. The chain: this device is talking to your home directly. Reliability: ● Offline · went offline 3 minutes ago. Relay Status: This Mac is serving Clitheroe Road, George Street and County Hall. All true from where each is standing; together they read as a disagreement.
  • “Offline” over a working home. Reliability's live status is the server's view of the cloud relay. The home was fine.
  • “Homes 0 · Accessories 0” directly under “serving three homes”. The counts filter to self-hosted homes and label the result “Homes”.
  • Nothing above the fold is for the person who tapped. Uptime, clients, subscriptions, webhooks, activity — a relay operator's dashboard, shown to someone who wanted to know if the lights would turn on.

Trimming inside each section is what #103 and #107 did, and the shape survived both. The shape is the problem.

The shape: an answer, then its evidence

The architecture already produces one verdict — statusPresentation() ranks the two facts (this device's link, the home's serving) to decide what the dot says. The card is that verdict, explained, in a fixed order. Nothing else gets a heading.

  1. VerdictThe home by name and whether it works, in the dot's own colour. Green: working as normal. Amber: working, but not the normal way — or slowly. Red: not working. Same ranking as the header dot, so the two can never disagree.
  2. BecauseOne sentence: why, and what happens next. This is where “while Homecast is open”, the takeover countdown and “under Apple Home's names” live — as clauses, not sections.
  3. Where — only when a hop is not greenThe chain from #38, unchanged. It is the best thing in the popover when something is broken and four green rows when nothing is. Healthy, it collapses to via Cloud relay · 14ms.
  4. DoAt most one thing. Reconnect now when this device's link is the fault; a quiet “nothing to do, Homecast has been notified” when it is the cloud relay; nothing at all when the home works.
  5. Then — two rows, not two sectionsReliability · 92% this week › and, on a relay Mac, This Mac · Standing by ›. History and duty, one line each, linking to the pages that already hold the full story. No live status here — the verdict owns “now”.

Three calls that are yours

  • Is “working on a backup path” amber or green? Today Local Mode is green (“your home works”) and Standby active is amber (“the cloud relay is offline”) — two colours for one situation. I've drawn both amber, because the caveat is real: on a phone it ends when the app closes, automations on the relay aren't running, and other people at home may not have the same access. If you'd rather it be green, both should be green. Consistency is the point, not the hue.
  • Relay duty comes off the dot. A self-hosted Mac that is standing by for another Mac shows an amber “Standby” pill today, over a home that works perfectly. In the proposal that is a green dot and a footer row. The code's own rationale already says duty “describes what this machine is doing rather than whether you can reach anything” — this follows it through.
  • The pill word for a backup path. Today two labels name two mechanisms: “Local Mode” and “Standby active”. One label for the situation reads better — I've used Standing in. “Local Mode” stays as the name of the mechanism and its settings page.

Found on the way and worth their own issues rather than a footnote: the second screenshot paints a 14.6s round trip green on the first hop — that reading is a missed-pong lower bound being shown as a fresh measurement, and the proposed cards use the classifier's verdict for that hop's colour instead of the raw number; and Homes 0 / Accessories 0 counts self-hosted homes under a label that says “Homes” — moot if the rows leave the popover, a bug if they don't.
